# Leveraged real estate, automated physical businesses and the 2030 house constraint (Toulouse, late October 2026)

Research date: 29 Oct 2026. Investor profile assumed: Johanna, salaried (CDI) Consulting Manager, net income assumed between 4,000 and 5,500 €/month, 50 k€ capital, saves 600 €/month, about 10 h/week, lives near Toulouse. Goals: a ~600 k€ family house in 2030 (with a mortgage), and 10 M€ gross wealth by 2036.
Source quality: most numbers on rates, tax and the market come from brokers, notaries, the press or official pages (service-public, Toulouse Métropole, photovoltaique.info). Most numbers on automated businesses come from **franchise or business-plan vendors**, which have a commercial bias. Each section below flags this.
Calculations marked "calc" are my own arithmetic. The script was 25-year amortizing loans with borrower insurance at 0.30%/yr on the initial capital.

## 1. French mortgage conditions late 2026, HCSF rules, and how a pre-2030 rental purchase affects the 600 k€ house

### Takeaway
Rates have been rising again since summer 2026: about 3.4% average on signed loans in September and about 3.55–3.65% on 20–25-year broker scales in October. HCSF rules are unchanged: 35% debt ratio including insurance, 25 years maximum, 20% flexibility. **On her salary alone (4–5.5 k€ net), Johanna can borrow only about 260–360 k€. Buying the 600 k€ house in 2030 requires roughly 8.4 k€/month of household net income for a ~550 k€ loan, or a very large down payment.** Buying a typical Toulouse rental before 2030 would cut her house borrowing capacity by about 140 k€ (≈40%) in a standard bank calculation.

### Cited Findings
**Rates**
- Observatoire Crédit Logement/CSA (signed loans, all durations): 3.40% average in September 2026, versus 3.31% in August. Published 15 Oct 2026. The October figure is due in mid-November. — reported via [Pretto analysis Oct 2026](https://www.pretto.fr/taux-immobilier/historique-taux-immobilier/2026/analyse-taux-immobilier-octobre-2026/) and [ABC Bourse – "les taux dépassent 3,50% en octobre"](https://www.abcbourse.com/marches/credit-immobilier-les-taux-depassent-3-50-en-octobre-et-une-nouvelle-hausse-s_703911)
- Meilleurtaux broker barometer, 12 Oct 2026, excluding insurance: 3.40% over 15 years, 3.55% over 20 years, 3.65% over 25 years. Pretto shows 3.66% over 20 years in October. Trend: rising; the ECB meets on 29 Oct 2026. — [Meilleurtaux barometer Oct 2026](https://www.meilleurtaux.com/credit-immobilier/taux-actuels/); [Pretto](https://www.pretto.fr/taux-immobilier/historique-taux-immobilier/2026/analyse-taux-immobilier-octobre-2026/)
- SeLoger: rates are "back to around 3.65% over 20 years" in autumn 2026, which could slow Toulouse's modest price gains. — [SeLoger – Prix immobiliers à Toulouse : bilan 2026](https://edito.seloger.com/node/23751)

**HCSF rules (legally binding since 2022)**
- Debt ratio capped at 35% of net income, borrower insurance included. Maximum term 25 years (27 years with a deferral for new builds/VEFA). Banks may exceed these rules on 20% of their quarterly production. — [CPIM – HCSF 2026](https://www.cpim.fr/hcsf-2026-plafond-endettement/); [Cafpi](https://www.cafpi.fr/credit-immobilier/actualites/credit-immobilier-le-changement-de-criteres-du-hcsf)
- In September 2026 the HCSF kept the 20% flexibility margin ("to support the recovery of mortgage lending"), kept the 35% / 25-year rules, and kept the countercyclical buffer at 1%. There was no loosening. — [Empruntis – HCSF maintien](https://www.empruntis.com/financement/actualites/2026/hcsf-maintien-credit-immo-20800/); [Stéphane Larue](https://stephanelarue.com/?p=205636); [Cafpi – HCSF 2026 pas d'assouplissement](https://cafpi.fr/credit-immobilier/actualites/hcsf-2026-pas-d-assouplissement-des-regles-du-credit-immobilier-ce-que-cela-change-pour-votre-projet)
- How the 20% flexibility is shared out: priority goes to main-residence buyers, especially first-time buyers. Non-main-residence purchases (rental investors) can use up to 30% of the flexibility, i.e. about 6% of total production (this rule dates from 2023). — [Selectra](https://selectra.info/finance/actualites/marche/credit-immobilier-endettement-35-derogations); [Monimmeuble](https://monimmeuble.com/?p=78992)
- Banks actually used 17.5% of the flexibility (reported in June 2026); another source reports 17.1% in Q4 2025. — [Meilleurtaux June 2026](https://www.meilleurtaux.com/credit-immobilier/actualites/2026-juin/credit-immobilier-les-derogations-montent-a-17-5.html); [Ateis Patrimoine](https://ateispatrimoine.com/credits/hcsf/)
- The HCSF confirmed it would not change how the debt ratio is calculated for investors. — [Cafpi HCSF 2026](https://cafpi.fr/credit-immobilier/actualites/hcsf-2026-pas-d-assouplissement-des-regles-du-credit-immobilier-ce-que-cela-change-pour-votre-projet)

**How rental income is counted (the "70% rule")**
- Weighting rental income at about 70% is **bank practice, not an HCSF rule**. Banks count 70–80% of gross rent depending on the institution (e.g. 800 € of rent counts as 560 €). — [Finalib](https://finalib.fr/blog/credit-financement/taux-endettement-35-pourcent-2026-contourner); [Hagnéré Patrimoine – capacité d'emprunt investissement locatif 2026](https://www.hagnere-patrimoine.fr/guides-patrimoine/investissement-immobilier/capacite-emprunt-investissement-locatif-2026)
- Since the 2022 HCSF framework, the "classic" method puts all loan payments in charges and rents (at 70%) in income. The older "differential" method counted only the investment's net cash flow; only some banks still use it. Worked example: income 5,000 €, main-residence loan 1,200 €, rental loan 700 €, rent 800 € (560 € retained) → (1,200+700)/(5,000+560) = **34.2%** with the classic method, versus **26.8%** with the differential method. — [Meilleurtaux 2022](https://www.meilleurtaux.com/rachat-de-credits/actualites/2022-avril/nouvelles-regles-hcsf-permettent-investisseurs-reinventer.html); [Finalib](https://finalib.fr/blog/credit-financement/taux-endettement-35-pourcent-2026-contourner)
- Self-employed or business income is usually counted only once there are **3 balance sheets** (often with at least 2 profitable). Self-employed people with under 3 years of activity may have their income counted at 0%. — [Portail Auto-Entrepreneur](https://www.portail-autoentrepreneur.fr/academie/developpement/conseils/pret-immobilier); [Pretto – taux d'endettement maximum](https://www.pretto.fr/capacite-demprunt/taux-dendettement-maximum)

**Transaction costs, Haute-Garonne**
- Haute-Garonne (31) is one of the départements that voted the +0.5 point rise in transfer duties (DMTO, from 4.5% to 5%). It applies from 2025 to March 2028. First-time buyers are exempt if the home is their main residence for at least 5 years. Sources disagree on the exact start date and on a possible 250 k€ cap on the exemption. — [Legifiscal](https://www.legifiscal.fr/actualites-fiscales/4107-frais-notaire-hausse-05-point-votee-27-departements.html?pdf=1); [The Local – map](https://www.thelocal.fr/20250305/map-where-in-france-are-notaire-fees-going-up); [Selectra](https://selectra.info/finance/actualites/marche/frais-notaire-primo-accedant-exoneration-hausse)

### Inferences
- **The 2030 house is a household-income question, not a strategy question.** A 600 k€ house costs about 648 k€ all-in (≈8% fees in the existing-home market; Haute-Garonne DMTO at 5%). On a single 4.5–5.5 k€ salary at today's rates she can borrow only 295–361 k€, so she would need about 290–350 k€ of own funds. The other route is a co-borrower: at 100 k€ down, a household needs about 8.4 k€/month net.
- Any rental loan taken before 2030 adds a full payment to the debt ratio but only 70% of the rent. That **permanently cuts house capacity by about 140 k€ per 170 k€ of rental debt** unless she finds a bank that uses the differential method. Sequencing matters: **house first (2030), rental leverage afterwards.** The other option is rentals so cash-flow-positive that 70% of rent covers the payment, which is unrealistic in Toulouse at 4.9% gross yield and ~3.65% rates.
- The 20% HCSF flexibility favours main-residence buyers. It is the lever for the house purchase (e.g. a slightly longer or higher-ratio loan), not for rentals.
- Business income (automated businesses) will not count for a 2030 mortgage unless the business has about 3 years of balance sheets, i.e. it would have to start by 2026–2027.

### Gaps
- No official Banque de France or HCSF page was fetched; HCSF facts come from broker summaries of the September 2026 communiqué.
- Exact borrower-insurance cost for her profile (age and health unknown). 0.30% is an assumption.
- Whether owning a rental removes "primo-accédant" status for the Haute-Garonne DMTO exemption (main-residence ownership history is what usually matters). To verify with a notary.
- Rate outlook to 2030 is unknown. The sensitivity table covers 3.3–4.0%.

## 2. Rental strategies and current tax rules (LMNP, LMP, SCI, location nue/Jeanbrun, short-term rental, DPE, BRRRR)

### Takeaway
Three tax changes matter. The 2025 finance law made furnished-rental (LMNP) resale costlier, because depreciation is now added back into the taxable gain. The 2026 social-security law raised social levies on LMNP rental profits to 18.6%; capital gains and bare-rental income stay at 17.2%. The 2026 finance law created the "Jeanbrun" landlord status, which allows depreciation in unfurnished rental, but only for **flats in collective buildings** and with rent caps. Short-term rental is heavily constrained in Toulouse: a permit is required, at most 2 permits per individual, and permanent conversions need compensation. The DPE energy-rating rental bans already cover G (2025) and will cover F (2028) and E (2034).

### Cited Findings
**LMNP (furnished rental, non-professional)**
- Depreciation deducted under the real-expense regime is added back when computing the capital gain on sales **since 15 Feb 2025** (art. 84 of finance law n°2025-127 of 14 Feb 2025, CGI art. 150 VB III). Student residences, senior residences and EHPAD nursing homes are excluded. — [Jedeclaremonmeuble](https://www.jedeclaremonmeuble.com/clause-grand-pere-lmnp/); [Assemblée nationale question](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/questions/QANR5L17QE11505.pdf); [Finalib](https://finalib.fr/blog/fiscalite/lmnp-2026-reforme-amortissements-plus-value)
- The 2026 finance law (loi n°2026-103 of 19 Feb 2026, in force 21 Feb 2026) **rejected** a 2% cap on LMNP depreciation, so depreciation itself is preserved. — [CPIM – loi de finances 2026](https://www.cpim.fr/loi-de-finances-2026/); [Investissement-locatif.com](https://www.investissement-locatif.com/amendement-lmnp-2026.html)
- The 2026 social-security law (LFSS, loi n°2025-1403 of 30 Dec 2025, art. 12) raised CSG on capital income by 1.4 points, to 10.6%, so total social levies go from **17.2% to 18.6%**. This applies to **LMNP rental profits**. **Bare-rental income (revenus fonciers) and private capital gains on real estate, including an LMNP resale, stay at 17.2%.** One source (Finalib) says real-estate gains are at 18.6%, but most sources disagree. — [Hagnéré – LFSS 2026 / LMNP](https://www.hagnere-patrimoine.fr/guides-patrimoine/investissement-immobilier/lfss-2026-prelevements-sociaux-186-lmnp-impact-rendement); [PAP](https://www.pap.fr/actualites/proprietaires-vous-echapperez-a-la-hausse-de-la-csg-en-2026-sauf-si/a29229); [Que Choisir](https://www.quechoisir.org/actualite-hausse-de-la-csg-vos-revenus-sont-ils-concernes-n173786/); conflicting: [Finalib](https://finalib.fr/blog/fiscalite/hausse-csg-2026-impact-capital-retraites)
- Selling quickly (before 10–15 years) is hit hardest by the add-back rule; long holding periods soften it through the holding-period allowances. — [Auguste Patrimoine](https://www.auguste-patrimoine.fr/blog/lmnp-en-2026-regime-fiscal-amortissements-et-fiscalite-a-la-revente)

**LMP (furnished rental, professional)**
- LMP status applies when the tax household's furnished-rental receipts exceed **23,000 €/year** and exceed the household's other activity income (salaries etc., CGI art. 155). — [Service-public (Entreprendre) – LMP](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/vosdroits/F32805?lang=fr); [Assemblée nationale answer](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/13/questions/QANR5L13QE44496.pdf)

**Unfurnished rental: Jeanbrun status (statut du bailleur privé, 2026 finance law art. 47)**
- Applies to purchases from 21 Feb 2026 to 31 Dec 2028 (for construction, the building-permit date counts). — [Meilleurtaux Placement – focus statut bailleur privé](https://placement.meilleurtaux.com/scpi/actualites/2026-fevrier/loi-de-finances-2026-focus-sur-le-nouveau-statut-du-bailleur-prive.html); [Actu-Juridique](https://www.actu-juridique.fr/fiscalite/fiscal-finances/location-nue-le-nouveau-dispositif-jeanbrun-en-faveur-des-bailleurs/)
- Eligible: **flats in collective residential buildings** (no geographic zoning), either new/VEFA, or existing homes with works ≥30% of the price that reach energy class A or B. Rented unfurnished as the tenant's main residence for **9 years**. Rents and tenant incomes must stay within caps: Pinel caps for "intermediate" rent, Loc'Avantages caps for social or very social rent. No renting to family. — [Meilleurtaux Placement](https://placement.meilleurtaux.com/scpi/actualites/2026-fevrier/loi-de-finances-2026-focus-sur-le-nouveau-statut-du-bailleur-prive.html)
- Annual depreciation rates: **new 3.5% / 4.5% / 5.5%** (intermediate / social / very social rent); **existing 3% / 3.5% / 4%**. Base = net price minus 20% for land. Lifetime cap: 80% of the net price. Annual cap: **8,000 € per tax household**, plus 2,000 € or 4,000 € if social or very social rents are at least 50% of rental income. Micro-foncier is excluded. At resale, depreciation reduces the acquisition price, so it raises the taxable gain. Depreciation is clawed back if the conditions are breached. — [Meilleurtaux Placement](https://placement.meilleurtaux.com/scpi/actualites/2026-fevrier/loi-de-finances-2026-focus-sur-le-nouveau-statut-du-bailleur-prive.html)
- **Rental-loss offset (déficit foncier)**: standard rules apply. A loss can be offset against global income up to **10,700 €/year** (21,400 € for energy renovation of a "thermal sieve" home), with a 10-year carry-forward. — same source
- A bill to loosen the eligibility conditions is pending (status unknown). — [Galivel CP mars 2026](https://www.galivel.com/media/files/260326_cp_positionnement_dispositif_jeanbrun.pdf)

**Short-term rental (loi Le Meur n°2024-1039 of 19 Nov 2024) and Toulouse rules**
- Micro-BIC for **unclassified** tourist lets: 30% allowance, receipts capped at **15,000 €**. **Classified** lets and chambres d'hôtes: 50% allowance up to 77,700 €. Applies to income from 2025 onward, declared in 2026. — [CPIM – Le Meur abattement 2026](https://www.cpim.fr/meuble-tourisme-loi-le-meur-abattement-micro-bic-2026/)
- New tourist lets that need a change-of-use permit in tense areas must have a DPE rated A to E; the worst-rated homes are phased out by 2034. — [Optimhome – loi Le Meur 2026](https://www.optimhome.com/fr/blog/location-meublee-touristique-2026-ce-que-change-la-loi-le-meur)
- From 20 May 2026, every tourist let in every commune must be declared and registered. A national e-service is announced for Q4 2026. — [Optimhome](https://www.optimhome.com/fr/blog/location-meublee-touristique-2026-ce-que-change-la-loi-le-meur) (one Toulouse-specific page says 1 Jan 2026; dates conflict)
- **Toulouse (official page, updated 1 Jan 2026)**:
  - Renting out one's main residence is allowed **up to 120 days/year**, with a registration number.
  - Secondary residences: a **temporary change-of-use permit without compensation, max 2 per individual**, valid 2 years and renewable, with stays of at most 90 consecutive days per occupant.
  - Legal entities, or individuals with more than 2 units: **permanent change of use with compensation**. This means converting non-residential space into housing, or buying "titres de commercialité".
  - Commercial premises can be authorized without compensation.
  - Fines: up to 10,000 € for exceeding 120 days, and up to 50,000 € plus 1,000 €/m²/day for housing without a change-of-use permit.
  - Rules: règlement of 1 Nov 2023.
  — [Toulouse Métropole – Louer un local meublé pour du tourisme](https://metropole.toulouse.fr/demarches/louer-un-local-meuble-pour-du-tourisme-ou-de-courtes-durees); [Règlement 01-11-2023 (PDF)](https://metropole.toulouse.fr/sites/toulouse-fr/files/2023-11/reglement-01-11-2023_0.pdf)

**DPE rental-ban calendar (mainland France)**
- G+ homes banned for new leases since 1 Jan 2023; **G since 1 Jan 2025; F from 1 Jan 2028; E from 1 Jan 2034**. Covers unfurnished and furnished leases. Rents on F and G homes have been frozen since Aug 2022. — [BailFacile](https://www.bailfacile.fr/guides/calendrier-interdiction-location-dpe); [Crédit Agricole e-immobilier](https://e-immobilier.credit-agricole.fr/conseils/reglementation/interdiction-location-dpe-calendrier)

**Value-add and refinancing ("BRRRR"-style buy, renovate, rent, refinance)**
- France's rechargeable mortgage (hypothèque rechargeable) lets the same collateral secure new loans up to the amount repaid, but **only if the original deed contains a recharge clause**. It is little used in practice and does not release equity "for free". — [Moneyvox](https://www.moneyvox.fr/immobilier/hypotheque-rechargeable.php); [Boursedescredits](https://www.boursedescredits.com/lexique-definition-pret-hypothecaire-hypotheque-rechargeable-3560.php); [Futura](https://www.futura-sciences.com/maison/questions-reponses/finances-quest-ce-quune-hypotheque-rechargeable-2796/)

### Inferences
- **US-style BRRRR (cash-out refinancing on a revalued property) is structurally weak in France.** Loans are amortizing and capped by HCSF on the borrower's income, not on the property's value. The French equivalents are financing works in the initial loan ("110%" loans), a mortgage-backed loan (prêt hypothécaire), or simply reselling (marchand de biens). This is my inference; no source states it directly.
- The Jeanbrun status cannot be used on a house: it applies only to flats in collective buildings. It suits a post-2030 Toulouse flat bought new or heavily renovated, not a near-term cash-flow play. The 8,000 €/yr depreciation cap limits the benefit for a single taxpayer.
- **LMNP remains the most tax-efficient regime for a salaried investor** in the short run, since depreciation largely offsets rental profit. The 18.6% levy only applies once the depreciation is used up, and resale is costlier since 2025.
- **LMP is out of reach before 2030** without very large rental receipts: furnished receipts would need to exceed her salary.
- Short-term rental in Toulouse is effectively capped at 2 secondary-residence units for an individual, and only with permits. Unclassified listings lose most of the micro-BIC benefit (30%, 15 k€ cap), so classification (≥50%) or the real regime is necessary.
- When buying any older rental, the 2028 (F) and 2034 (E) bans force energy works to be budgeted. That is an opportunity (discounts on F/G "passoires") and a capex risk.

### Gaps
- **SCI à l'IS (company subject to corporate tax): not researched in this session.** The commonly cited regime, to verify: corporate tax at 15% up to 42,500 € of profit, 25% above; depreciation allowed; gain taxed on net book value; dividends taxed again. Its effect on personal borrowing capacity (personal guarantees) is also not researched.
- Micro-BIC for **long-term** furnished lets after the Le Meur law: assumed unchanged at 50% / 77,700 €, but not verified this session.
- Le Meur: whether Toulouse has used the option to cut the main-residence cap from 120 to 90 days. The Toulouse page still says 120 days. The exact 2034 DPE threshold for tourist lets (A–D?) is not confirmed.
- The DPE reform of 1 Jan 2026 (new electricity coefficient) reportedly took about 850,000 homes out of F/G. It was seen in a search snippet but not confirmed from a primary source.
- Colocation (shared-flat) yields and rules in Toulouse: no specific source found.

## 3. Toulouse area market data 2025–2026

### Takeaway
Toulouse is flat. As of 1 Sept 2026, apartments average **~3,570 €/m²** (+0.7% in 1 year, +1.1% in 5 years) and houses **~4,110 €/m²** (+0.3% in 1 year, **-5% in 3 years**). Gross yield on a T2 is about **4.9%**. A 600 k€ budget buys about 140–180 m² of house depending on the commune. Leverage cannot rely on capital gains here.

### Cited Findings
- **Toulouse city (SeLoger–Meilleurs Agents, 1 Sept 2026):**
  - Apartments: **3,569 €/m²**, +0.7% over 1 year, +2.6% over 3 years, +1.1% over 5 years. 1.7% below the June 2022 peak; +24.7% since 2018.
  - Houses: **4,114 €/m²**, +0.3% over 1 year, **-5.0% over 3 years, -3.2% over 5 years**.
  - Rents: 15.27 €/m² (flats), 12.69 €/m² (houses). **Gross yield on a T2: 4.9%**. Average time to sell: 67 days.
  - National outlook for 2027: +2–3% possible; no Toulouse-specific forecast.
  — [SeLoger – Prix immobiliers à Toulouse : bilan 2026 et perspectives 2027](https://edito.seloger.com/node/23751)
- Nearby communes, apartment €/m² and 1-year change: Colomiers 2,458 € (-6.3%), Tournefeuille 2,887 € (-6.3%), Blagnac 3,024 € (-6.3%), Muret 2,428 € (+8.9%). — [SeLoger](https://edito.seloger.com/node/23751)

### Inferences
- What 600 k€ buys (calc): about **146 m²** at the Toulouse house average (4,114 €/m²), about **140–145 m²** in Balma's sought-after areas, and about **167–182 m²** in Tournefeuille.
- With flat or falling prices (houses -5% over 3 years) and rates up again, **waiting until 2030 carries little price risk** on current evidence. This is not a forecast.
- A 4.9% gross yield against a 3.55–3.65% mortgage rate plus ~0.3% insurance gives **negative leveraged cash flow** on standard Toulouse flats with 20–25-year amortization (see the -400 €/month example in section 1).

### Gaps
- No notary (Perval / Notaires du Grand Sud) data was found in this session. All figures are from platforms using DVF or algorithmic estimates.
- No reliable 2026 figures for L'Union or Colomiers house prices.
- No sourced gross yields for nearby mid-size towns (Montauban, Albi, Castres, Carcassonne), which are usually cited as higher-yield. Not verified.

## 4. Realistic 10-year wealth multiples from leveraged French real estate starting with ~50 k€, and marchand de biens basics

### Takeaway
Under HCSF rules and Toulouse's 4.9% yields, a salaried buy-and-hold investor starting with 50 k€ can realistically build **a few hundred thousand euros of net equity in 10 years**. **That is about 2–10× the starting 50 k€, not the ~200× needed for 10 M€.** Property dealing (marchand de biens) gives higher returns per deal but is an active, taxed trading business, not passive income.

### Cited Findings
- Price growth assumption anchors: Toulouse houses -3.2% and flats +1.1% over 5 years; national 2027 outlook +2–3%. — [SeLoger](https://edito.seloger.com/node/23751)
- Rates of about 3.55–3.65% over 20–25 years (Oct 2026). — [Meilleurtaux](https://www.meilleurtaux.com/credit-immobilier/taux-actuels/)
- **Marchand de biens (property dealer):**
  - Committing in the purchase deed to resell **within 5 years** (CGI art. 1115) cuts transfer duties to about **0.715%**, versus about 5.8% normally. If the resale deadline is missed, the full duties plus late interest become due, and every lot must be resold.
  - VAT on the margin applies when buying from a non-taxable seller such as a private individual.
  - The tax authority can reclassify an individual as a professional dealer. There is no fixed threshold; one source cites 3–4 short-hold operations in 5 years as a risk zone.
  — [Hayot Expertise – TVA marchand de biens 2026](https://hayot-expertise.fr/blog/tva-immobiliere-marchand-de-biens-2026); [Jedeclaremonmeuble – frais de notaire revente 5 ans](https://www.jedeclaremonmeuble.com/frais-notaire-revente-5-ans/); [Finalib – marchand de biens 2026](https://finalib.fr/blog/fiscalite/marchand-de-biens-2026-statut-fiscalite-rentabilite)

### Inferences
Worked examples (calc). Loans are 25-year amortizing at 3.65%; 29.6% of principal is repaid after 10 years and 10.6% after 4 years.
- **(a) One Toulouse T2, buy-and-hold** (172 k€ all-in, 100% financed, -400 €/month cash flow):

| Price growth | Equity after 10 years | Net of cash injected (≈48 k€ of negative cash flow) |
|---|---|---|
| 0%/yr | 150 − 121 = ~29 k€ | ~-19 k€ |
| 2%/yr | 183 − 121 = ~62 k€ | ~+14 k€ |

  **Without value-add or a lower purchase price, a standard Toulouse rental barely beats saving the cash.**
- **(b) Aggressive portfolio: 1 M€ of property bought at once** (1.1 M€ financed including fees). This is not feasible within HCSF limits on her income; it is shown only as an upper bound. Debt after 10 years is about 775 k€. Equity is 225 k€ at 0%/yr, 444 k€ at 2%/yr, 569 k€ at 3%/yr, before cumulative cash flows. At Toulouse yields, cash flow would be about -2.3 k€/month, which is impossible on 600 €/month of savings.
- **(c) What is realistically achievable.** Value-add (works, splitting units, colocation) plus higher-yield secondary towns plus disciplined sequencing (house first). This could plausibly turn 50 k€ + 600 €/month into **~300–800 k€ of net real-estate equity by 2036**, i.e. a 3–8× multiple on total contributions (≈122 k€). This is a judgment range, not a sourced figure.
- **Reaching 10 M€ gross wealth by 2036 through French leveraged real estate is not realistic.** HCSF caps debt on salary income, Toulouse yields are below the cost of debt, and capital gains are flat. Even 10 M€ of *gross assets* (counting debt-financed property at full value) would need about 8–10 M€ of debt, far beyond what a single salary can carry. Note "gross" wealth: a highly leveraged portfolio inflates gross assets but requires the investor to become a professional (LMP / company structure, commercial-property-style financing).
- Property dealing could compound faster (e.g. 15–25% gross margin per deal in good conditions; unsourced). But each deal needs active work (sourcing, works, sale) beyond 10 h/week at scale, carries execution risk, and dealer profits are taxed as business income or corporate profit, not as private capital gains.

### Gaps
- No sourced French studies of realized 10-year returns for leveraged small investors.
- No sourced typical gross margins for marchands de biens in Occitanie.

## 5. Automated / unstaffed physical businesses in France: capex, revenue, net yields, time, financing, franchises, risks

### Takeaway
Most "automated" businesses earn **mid-to-high single-digit net yields on capex**. They need site-specific diligence and 1–5 h/week of supervision (cleaning, maintenance, cash, customer issues). Their headline profitability numbers come mostly from **franchisors and business-plan vendors** (biased upward). Two structural negatives as of 2026:
- **Rooftop solar** for third-party investors has largely collapsed: the June 2026 tariff reform reportedly cut the ≤100 kWc tariff to 1.1 c€/kWh.
- **Self-storage in Occitanie** is already the densest market in France.

### Cited Findings
**Self-storage (box / container)**
- France: 2,129 facilities and about 496,000–497,750 units (2025 survey by Location-gardemeuble.fr). Ten chains hold about 45% of units; independents about 34.5%. 639 sites are container-based, which is "often cheaper but not always compliant with European norms". **Occitanie has the highest density in France: 9.5 units per 1,000 residents, versus 7 nationally.** — [Inside Self Storage, Dec 2025](https://www.insideselfstorage.com/market-conditions/the-french-self-storage-market-what-2025-data-shows-about-the-country-s-exceptional-growth); [FEDESSA French market study 2025](https://www.fedessa.org/resource/french-self-storage-market-study-2025-pdf.html)
- Europe 2025 (FEDESSA/CBRE): about 10,571 sites (+7.3%), average occupancy about 78%, revenue per m² +5.4%. — [Modern Storage Media](https://www.modernstoragemedia.com/news/fedessa-releases-2025-european-self-storage-industry-report)
- Claimed French market growth of 15–20%/yr; average occupancy 86%; average rent about 268 €/m²/yr (2024, attributed to FEDESSA). Gross yields 6–11% (blogs) or 7.5–10%. Buying individual boxes costs 5–25 k€. — [Le Frugalisme](https://www.lefrugalisme.com/investir-box-de-stockage/); [Culture Financière](https://culture-financiere.com/?p=1970); [AC Franchise – Onebox](https://ac-franchise.com/article/onebox-la-franchise-qui-surfe-sur-le-boom-du-self-stockage)
- Franchises:
  - Storebox: 30 k€ entry fee plus about 60 k€ investment. — [Observatoire de la Franchise – Storebox](https://www.observatoiredelafranchise.fr/creation-entreprise/franchise-storebox-5147.htm)
  - SOS Box: from 35 k€, no entry fee, given an existing plot or building. — [Observatoire de la Franchise – SOS Box](https://www.observatoiredelafranchise.fr/creation-entreprise/franchise-sos-box-2071.htm)
  - Box en Loc: capex depends on site size and rehabilitation works. — [Observatoire – Box en Loc](https://www.observatoiredelafranchise.fr/creation-entreprise/franchise-box-en-loc-5044.htm)
  - Franchisor claims of "15% ROI, break-even in 6–18 months" are vendor claims. — [AC Franchise](https://ac-franchise.com/article/onebox-la-franchise-qui-surfe-sur-le-boom-du-self-stockage)
- Homebox (a leading operator) says the market is "ever more competitive" (March 2026). — [Homebox press release (PDF)](https://res.cloudinary.com/darhmutay/raw/upload/v1774298752/strapi-assets/production/CP_HOMEBOX_franchisemars26_6fd3481cfa.pdf)

**Self-service laundries (laveries automatiques)**
- More than 4,500 laundries in France in 2025; sector sales about 400 M€. Annual revenue per laundry is 20–120 k€, often about 30–35 k€ on average. Net margin 15–25% (or 20–35%). Rent is 20–30% of revenue, water and energy 20–25%. Capex 20–150 k€. Break-even in 3–5 years. Franchise: entry fee 15–25 k€, royalty 4–6% of revenue, personal contribution 15–50 k€. — [Modèles de Business Plan](https://modelesdebusinessplan.com/blogs/infos/investir-laverie-automatique); [HelloPro](https://conseils.hellopro.fr/quelle-est-la-rentabilite-d-une-laverie-automatique-5396.html); [Le Blog du Dirigeant](https://www.leblogdudirigeant.com/ouvrir-laverie-automatique-budget-rentabilite/); [SeLoger – Investir dans une laverie](https://edito.seloger.com/conseils-d-experts/investir/investir-une-laverie-automatique-article-20992.html)

**Car-wash stations**
- Capex 80–500 k€; a small station with one high-pressure bay plus a gantry costs about 340 k€. A 4-bay station turns over about 100–150 k€ excl. VAT/yr (or 100–200 k€ for a medium station). One high-pressure bay brings about 2,500 €/month. Energy, water and detergent are about 12% of revenue; maintenance about 10%. Net margin 10–25% (claims of "80% margin" refer to gross margin). Personal contribution from about 50 k€. — [HelloPro](https://conseils.hellopro.fr/combien-rapporte-une-station-de-lavage-3913.html); [Toute la Franchise – lavage auto 2026](https://www.toute-la-franchise.com/vie-de-la-franchise-A27011-ouvrir-station-lavage-franchise.html); [Modèles de BP – marché](https://modelesdebusinessplan.com/blogs/infos/marche-station-lavage-auto-chiffres); [Copeps](https://copeps.fr/actualites/franchise-lavage-auto-automatique/)
- Eléphant Bleu reported its "best year in 10 years" (trade press). — [L'Argus](https://www.largus.fr/pros/actualite-automobile/la-meilleure-annee-delephant-bleu-depuis-10-ans-5401379.html)

**EV charging points**
- **202,804** public charging points in France (Sept 2026); 200,926 at 31 Aug 2026 (Avere-France barometer). Average use rose **from 25.2 to 32.7 sessions per point per month** (Sept 2025 → Sept 2026, +30%). Technical availability is about 89%. **High-power chargers take 5–10 years to pay back depending on traffic.** Rural and peri-urban sites run below average use, which deepens operator losses. — [Économie Matin](https://www.economiematin.fr/?p=234369); [Automobile Propre](https://www.automobile-propre.com/articles/cest-fait-la-france-passe-le-cap-des-200-000-points-de-recharge-ouverts-au-public/); [Avere-France press release](https://www.avere-france.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Communique-de-presse_Avere-France_Rentree_2026.pdf); [Rouleur Électrique](https://rouleur-electrique.fr/recharge-electrique-en-france-juillet-2026-bat-tous-les-records-dutilisation-des-bornes/)

**Rooftop solar (installing on a rented roof as third-party investor; or leasing out one's own roof)**
- The S21 tariff order was amended by the **order of 1 June 2026, in force 5 June 2026**, for building, hangar and carport installations. Total sale requires at least 9 kWc. — [photovoltaique.info – arrêté en vigueur](https://www.photovoltaique.info/fr/tarifs-dachat-et-autoconsommation/tarifs-dachat/arrete-tarifaire-en-vigueur/)

Before 5 June 2026, total sale for 9–36 kWc was about 8.05 c€. — [photovoltaique.info – arrêté S21 modifications 2025](https://www.photovoltaique.info/fr/actualites/detail/arrete-s21-details-des-modifications-de-larrete-s21-et-publication-des-nouvelles-grilles-tarifaires/); [Hellowatt](https://www.hellowatt.fr/panneaux-solaires-photovoltaiques/tarif-rachat-photovoltaique)

Minimum roof of about 500–1,000 m². — [Les Énergies Renouvelables – location toiture](https://www.les-energies-renouvelables.eu/conseils/panneaux-solaires-pour-entreprise-professionnels/location-toiture-photovoltaique)

**Parking spaces / garage boxes**
- Gross yields: 4–5% in Paris and 5–8% elsewhere (Finary). 6–10% (SeLoger, Calcunet). **8–9% in Rennes, Grenoble or Toulouse** (one site, Aug 2026). Closed boxes yield 5–7% and rent 20–40% above open spaces. Net yields are materially lower after property tax, vacancy and management. — [Finary](https://finary.com/fr/blog/immobilier/locatif/investir-parking); [SeLoger – garage](https://edito.seloger.com/conseils-d-experts/investir/investir-un-garage-rentable-article-22031.html); [Investissement-locatif-avis (Aug 2026)](https://investissement-locatif-avis.fr/investir-parking-rentabilite-prix/); [CPIM parking 2026](https://www.cpim.fr/investir-parking-grande-ville-rendement-strategies-2026/); [Optimhome](https://www.optimhome.com/fr/blog/investir-dans-un-parking-ou-un-box-la-rentabilite-meconnue)

- **Pizza:** machine capex 30–90 k€ HT (a parliamentary answer cites about 40 k€ HT). Vendor-cited sales 6–18 k€/month (8–12 k€ for artisan pizza). Site rent 200–600 €/month or a 10–25% commission. Monthly charges are 60–75% of sales; net margin 25–40%. Location drives everything: stations 7.6–10.2 k€/month versus about 1.5 k€ in residential areas. — [HelloPro – distributeur pizzas](https://conseils.hellopro.fr/quelle-est-la-rentabilite-d-un-distributeur-automatique-de-pizzas-5612.html); [Modèles de BP – distributeur](https://modelesdebusinessplan.com/blogs/infos/combien-rapporte-distributeur-automatique); [Assemblée nationale (older answer)](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/15/questions/QANR5L15QE40198.pdf)

## 6. Which of these can produce cash flow toward the 2030 house without hurting borrowing capacity?

### Takeaway
Two things protect the 2030 house: **cash** (bigger down payment) and **a clean debt ratio**. Low-capex, **debt-free** assets bought with part of the 50 k€ do not touch the HCSF ratio: one or two parking boxes, a used bread machine at a good site, or a minority stake in a small storage site. Any personal loan before 2030, whether rental or business, reduces house capacity almost euro for euro. Business income will not count for a 2030 mortgage unless it shows about 3 years of balance sheets.

### Cited Findings
- The debt ratio counts every loan payment in full but only about 70% of rents, under bank practice since the 2022 HCSF framework. — [Finalib](https://finalib.fr/blog/credit-financement/taux-endettement-35-pourcent-2026-contourner); [Meilleurtaux](https://www.meilleurtaux.com/rachat-de-credits/actualites/2022-avril/nouvelles-regles-hcsf-permettent-investisseurs-reinventer.html)
- Business or self-employed income is usually counted only after 3 balance sheets (often 2 of them profitable). — [Portail Auto-Entrepreneur](https://www.portail-autoentrepreneur.fr/academie/developpement/conseils/pret-immobilier); [Pretto](https://www.pretto.fr/capacite-demprunt/taux-dendettement-maximum)
- Parking gross yields: 5–8% outside Paris, 8–9% cited for Toulouse. — [Finary](https://finary.com/fr/blog/immobilier/locatif/investir-parking); [Investissement-locatif-avis](https://investissement-locatif-avis.fr/investir-parking-rentabilite-prix/)
- The HCSF 20% flexibility mainly serves main-residence and first-time buyers. — [Selectra](https://selectra.info/finance/actualites/marche/credit-immobilier-endettement-35-derogations)

### Inferences
- **Best fit before 2030 (debt-free, low-time):**
  - **Parking/garage boxes in Toulouse bought cash.** About 4–7% net; liquid enough to sell for the 2030 down payment; no debt-ratio impact; rents count at 70% in the bank's income.
  - **Small cash-funded vending or a share of a self-storage site**, with no personal loan. Higher risk.
- **To avoid before 2030:**
  - Leveraged Toulouse rentals: about -140 k€ of house capacity per 172 k€ loan, and negative cash flow of about -400 €/month.
  - Leveraged car wash or laundry where she personally guarantees loans or borrows personally.
  - Third-party rooftop solar: the tariff has collapsed.
- **Possible exception:** a business started in 2026–2027 inside a company, financed by equipment leasing **without personal debt**. If it is profitable for 3 years by 2030, its income could start to count. This depends on the bank, and many newcos require a founder guarantee, which some banks treat as a commitment.
- **What really moves the 2030 needle: cash.** 50 k€ plus 600 €/month for 4 years gives about 79 k€ before returns (calc). At ~5% that grows to roughly 88 k€. That is enough for notary fees (~48 k€) plus a small down payment, but the 600 k€ house still needs about 8.4 k€/month of household net income (or a co-borrower) at current rates.
- After the 2030 house, rental leverage (LMNP, Jeanbrun on a renovated Toulouse flat, or higher-yield towns) becomes the logical next phase. It would be bounded by the residual debt ratio after the house loan: probably little room until salary or rental income rises.

### Gaps
- How Toulouse-area banks treat a founder's personal guarantee on a company loan in the 35% calculation: no source found.
- Real net yields for Toulouse parking or boxes after the 2026 property tax: no dedicated Toulouse source beyond a single aggregator.
