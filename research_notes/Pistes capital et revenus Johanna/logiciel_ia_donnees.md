# Software / SaaS / AI-agent / data-product path for a part-time solo founder in France (state as of Oct 2026)

Profile reminder: Johanna is a salaried Consulting Manager at Sopra Steria Next in Toulouse. She has 12 years in aerospace B2B (Airbus customer services, export control, pilot training, MRO apps) and works in product, pricing, go-to-market and data. She is not a developer. She has 50 k€ of capital plus 600 €/month and about 10 h/week. Her target is 10 M€ gross wealth by 2036.
Research date: 29 Oct 2026. Older data is flagged with its year. "Secondary" means an aggregator or blog repeating a primary source that I could not open.

## 1. What 2025-2026 data say about outcomes for solo or bootstrapped micro-SaaS and vertical SaaS (time to 10k / 100k / 1M ARR, success share, revenue distribution)

### Takeaway
Most independent SaaS stays tiny. In MicroConf's 2024 survey, 28% of products made under $1k MRR and 65% had 1-10 paying customers. Among companies that already monetize and use ChartMogul, only 13.4% reach $1M ARR within 3 years and 25.1% within 5 years. About half get there within 10 years, and about 1 in 10 reaches $10M ARR. AI-native products reach early milestones faster, but they churn faster too. A part-time founder sits below these medians: full-time founders grow about 30% faster.

### Cited Findings
**MicroConf State of Independent SaaS (2024 edition, 469 founders, data collected late 2023; I found no 2025 or 2026 edition on microconf.com)**
- 28% of independent SaaS companies make under $1,000 MRR, the largest single group. 65% have only 1-10 paying customers, and more than half have fewer than 50. — [MicroConf $0-10K ARR page](https://microconf.com/founders/0-10k-arr)
- Full-time founders grow 30% faster than part-time founders, per report p.45. Another MicroConf page says "2.2x", so the figure is inconsistent. — [MicroConf $0-10K ARR page](https://microconf.com/founders/0-10k-arr); [MicroConf $100K-1M page](https://microconf.com/founders/100k-1m-arr)
- SEO and word of mouth are the highest-impact marketing channels for indie SaaS. The figure is garbled on the page. — [MicroConf $0-10K ARR page](https://microconf.com/founders/0-10k-arr)
- The 2024 report sits behind a form. The landing page shows only the 2024 title and the 2020-2022 archive. — [MicroConf State of Indie SaaS](https://microconf.com/state-of-indie-saas)
- Freemius (2025) attributes "median profitable micro-SaaS ≈ $4.2K MRR, ~700 founders surveyed" to MicroConf. I could not check this against MicroConf, and the Freemius page did not load. — [Freemius, Micro-SaaS in 2025](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/)

**ChartMogul "Against the Odds: 2025 SaaS Growth Report" (15 Oct 2025, 6,525 software companies, clock starts at first monetization)**
- Almost half reach $1M ARR within 10 years (cohorts 2016 or earlier). About 1 in 10 reach $10M ARR, and about 1 in 50 (≈2.3%) reach $25M ARR. — [ChartMogul 2025](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-growth-the-odds-of-making-it/)
- The typical time to $1M ARR is 2-5 years. Share reaching $1M ARR: 3.3% in under 1 year, 13.4% in under 3 years, 25.1% within 5 years. — [ChartMogul 2025](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-growth-the-odds-of-making-it/)
- The 2021 cohort was the weakest, with 10.1% reaching $1M within 3 years, against about 15% for the 2016-2019 cohorts. The 2023 cohort was about 50% more likely to reach $1M within 6 months. — [ChartMogul 2025](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-growth-the-odds-of-making-it/)
- AI-native companies were 3x more likely to reach $1M ARR within 6 months and 8x more likely to reach $10M within 12 months. Still, fewer than 1% of AI-native companies reach $10M within 12 months. Vertical SaaS and PLG were less likely to become outliers. ChartMogul also notes that fast acquisition can come with fast churn. — [ChartMogul 2025](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-growth-the-odds-of-making-it/)
- A linked ChartMogul report says "only 13%" reach $10M ARR within 10 years, against "~1 in 10" here, so the methodologies differ. — [ChartMogul 2025](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-growth-the-odds-of-making-it/)
- Older data (2023): the median company takes about 2 years 9 months to go from first paying customer to $1M ARR, and best-in-class takes 9 months. — [ChartMogul SaaS Growth Report 2023](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-growth-report/)
- Older data (2024): top-quartile bootstrapped companies reach $1M ARR in about 2 years, only 4 months slower than VC-backed ones. — [ChartMogul Bootstrapped vs VC 2024 (PDF)](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-growth-vc-bootstrapped/saas-growth-vc-bootstrapped-2024.pdf)

**Stripe**
- Older data (2024): the top 100 AI companies on Stripe took a median 11.5 months to reach $1M annualized revenue, about 4 months faster than the fastest-growing SaaS cohort (≈15 months). — [Stripe blog](https://stripe.com/blog/inside-the-growth-of-the-top-ai-companies-on-stripe)
- In its 2025 annual letter, Stripe reports that 20% of new Atlas companies charged their first customer within 30 days, against 8% in 2020. Twice as many young startups hit $10M ARR within 3 months as in 2024. Stripe gave no exact counts. — [secondary summary of Stripe 2025 letter](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/stripe-2025-annual-letter-key-takeaways)
- Stripe economics analysis (Oct 2026) of AI application companies that signed up from Jan 2023 to Jun 2025 and earned ≥$100k cumulative revenue: US firms were 2.2x as likely as EU firms to reach $1M within 12 months. — [PPC Land (secondary)](https://ppc.land/us-ai-app-firms-2-2x-likelier-than-eu-peers-to-reach-1m-stripe-finds/); [Stripe, Indexing the AI economy](https://stripe.com/guides/indexing-the-ai-economy)

### Inferences
- **Realistic distribution for a part-time solo non-developer.** The median outcome is below $1-4k MRR. A $10k MRR product (≈120 k€ ARR) is already roughly a top-quartile indie outcome. A €1M ARR product is a top-decile outcome, and in ChartMogul's sample, which already excludes products that never monetized, it typically takes 2-5 years after first revenue. With ~10 h/week, Johanna should assume the slower end of that range.
- **Implied timeline to 2036.** If she reaches first revenue in 2027, the plausible windows are €10k ARR in 2027-28, €100k ARR in 2028-30, and €1M ARR in 2030-33 in the good scenario. That is roughly the latest point that still allows a meaningful exit before 2036.
- **What AI-native speed means here.** Speed is real at the top, but the same report links it to churn. In vertical B2B (aerospace), the sales cycle and trust set the pace, not the build.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025 or 2026 MicroConf data could be checked; the report is gated. No Indie Hackers dataset with distributions was found.
- No Baremetrics 2025-26 open benchmark was retrieved.
- No source isolates part-time founders' time to $10k/$100k ARR. The only data point is the "30% faster" figure for full-timers.
- Bpifrance, Maddyness and Les Echos data on French bootstrapped SaaS outcomes were not found in this pass.

## 2. Valuation multiples for small SaaS / AI products (2025-2026) and the ARR needed for a ~5-10 M€ exit

### Takeaway
Below about $1M ARR or $1M profit, small SaaS sells on profit: about 3.9x confirmed net income on Acquire.com in 2024 and 2025, or about 1-3x revenue. ARR multiples (about 3-5.5x for $3-10M ARR, about 4.8x modeled for bootstrapped companies in Jan 2025) only apply at larger sizes. A 5 M€ exit therefore needs about 1.2-2.5 M€ ARR, and a 10 M€ exit about 2-4 M€ ARR, unless the business shows strong growth or NRR or an unusual strategic premium.

### Cited Findings
**Acquire.com, Acquisition Multiples Report, "2025 findings" (PDF published Feb 2026; mostly deals under $10M EV)**
- Average public SaaS revenue multiple: 17x (2022), 7x (2023), 7x (2024), 5.5x (2025). — [Acquire.com Multiples Report 2025 (PDF)](https://blog.acquire.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Acquire-Multiples-Report-2025.pdf)
- Confirmed SaaS purchase price / net income: 4.3x in 2023 (145 deals), 3.9x in 2024 (205 deals), 3.9x in 2025 (129 deals). By size in 2025: 3.7x under $100k profit, 3.9x for $100k-$1M profit. — [same PDF](https://blog.acquire.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Acquire-Multiples-Report-2025.pdf)
- Median days on market is 90; average is 81 (185 deals). Average profit margin of profitable SaaS listed: 67% (2023), 71% (2024), 71% (2025). — [same PDF](https://blog.acquire.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Acquire-Multiples-Report-2025.pdf)
- Share of serious buyer interest by asking price vs fair market value: 2% at +10%, 30% at +5%, 60% at FMV, 80% at -5%, 92% at -10%. — [same PDF](https://blog.acquire.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Acquire-Multiples-Report-2025.pdf)
- Benchmark against other marketplaces: profit multiples are 0.6-49.9x for deals under $50k, 1.4-2.8x for $50-250k and 3.3-4.5x for $250k+. A second marketplace reports EBITDA multiples of 5-7x under $2M EBITDA and 7-10x above $2M. — [same PDF](https://blog.acquire.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Acquire-Multiples-Report-2025.pdf)
- Acquire's guidance: many SaaS deals trade at about 3-5x annual profit or 1-3x annual revenue. — [Acquire blog, SaaS valuation multiples 2026](https://blog.acquire.com/saas-valuation-multiples/) (via search snippet; the page did not render)
- A third-party analysis of about 615 Acquire listings found average asking multiples of about 2.4x revenue and 7.4x profit. These are asking prices, not closed deals. — [BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/saas-valuation-multiples-2026)

**Larger brokers and indices**
- SaaS Capital (Jan 2025): modeled private multiple of 4.8x ARR for bootstrapped and 5.3x for equity-backed companies. The SaaS Capital Index was about 7.0x run-rate revenue in early 2025. Secondary sources disagree on 2026 public levels (≈3.1x vs 4.2x ARR). — [SaaS Capital 2025](https://www.saas-capital.com/blog-posts/private-saas-company-valuations-multiples/); [ScaleXP](https://www.scalexp.com/blog/saas-benchmarks-arr-valuation/)
- FE International (via secondary): below about $1M ARR, valuation uses SDE at about 2.5-4x. ARR multiples are about 3-4.5x for $3-5M ARR, 3.5-5.5x for $5-10M ARR (8-12x EBITDA), and 5-7x above $20M ARR. Sources disagree on whether the SDE-to-ARR switch happens at $3M or $5M. — [FE International blog](https://www.feinternational.com/blog/saas-valuation-multiples) (figures relayed by [saasvaluationmultiple.com](https://saasvaluationmultiple.com/by-revenue) and others; not verified on FE's page)
- Premium multiples of 6-8x ARR are cited for growth above 30%, NRR above 110% and Rule of 40 above 50. — [ScaleXP (secondary)](https://www.scalexp.com/blog/saas-benchmarks-arr-valuation/)
- MicroConf pages state that 60% of indie SaaS exits price at 1x-3x forward ARR (2024 data). — [MicroConf $100K-1M page](https://microconf.com/founders/100k-1m-arr) (via search summary; not checked on the page)
- Since 8 Sep 2026, Empire Flippers lists annual multiples; earlier monthly multiples divide by 12. Older SaaS listings showed about 29-46x monthly profit (≈2.4-3.8x annual). — [Empire Flippers](https://empireflippers.com/switching-to-annual-multiples/)

**Strategic outlier**
- Base44 (Israel) sold to Wix for $80M cash in June 2025, six months after founding. The founder had 8 employees, who shared $25M as retention, and earn-outs run through 2029. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/18/six-month-old-solo-owned-vibe-coder-base44-sells-to-wix-for-80m-cash). Base44 later reported $50M ARR (Nov 2025) and $100M ARR (Mar 2026) per CTech. — [Calcalist tag page](https://www.calcalistech.com/tags/Base44)

### Inferences (exit math, euros ≈ dollars for simplicity)
- **Under 1 M€ ARR, the business sells on profit.** With a 71% margin, 1 M€ ARR gives about 0.7 M€ profit, and at 3.9-4.5x that is roughly **2.7-3.2 M€**.
- **For a 5 M€ exit**, she needs about 1.2-1.7 M€ ARR at 3-4x ARR with clean metrics, or about 2-2.5 M€ ARR at a conservative 2-2.5x.
- **For a 10 M€ exit**, she needs about 2-2.5 M€ ARR at 4-5x (growth above 30%, NRR above 110%), or 3-4 M€ ARR at 2.5-3.5x.
- **The strategic-buyer route is plausible in her domain but not something to plan on.** Aerospace and compliance software incumbents could pay above-market multiples for a niche product with sticky regulated-industry customers.
- **10 M€ gross wealth by 2036 via SaaS alone** requires roughly 2-4 M€ ARR by about 2034. That is a top-few-percent outcome, and very unlikely at 10 h/week. A more realistic SaaS contribution is a 1-3 M€ exit plus cash flow in the good scenario. This should be combined with other wealth levers.

### Gaps
- No 2026 primary dataset splits private SaaS multiples by ARR band, and FE's figures could not be verified on FE's site.
- No Europe/France-specific small-SaaS multiple data was found. French tax on exit (PFU, apport-cession 150-0 B ter) was not researched here.
- No dataset isolates AI-wrapper product multiples against classic SaaS for small deals.

## 3. How feasible it is to build with AI coding tools, no-code and freelancers as a non-developer in 10 h/week, and what it costs

### Takeaway
Shipping an MVP is now cheap and fast. Lovable and Bolt start at about $25/month, Lovable's Business plan is $50/month, and 20% of new Stripe Atlas companies charge a customer within 30 days. The hard part is the last 20%: security, multi-tenant data isolation, integrations and reliability. Vibe-coded apps have had well-documented data leaks. For B2B aerospace or compliance buyers, she should budget a paid developer or security review and treat AI-built code as a prototype.

### Cited Findings
- Lovable: entry plan about $25/month; free tier 5 credits/day (30/month max); Business plan $50/month adds SSO and a security center. Bolt.new: free tier 1M tokens/month; Pro $25/month. Usage is metered through credits or tokens, so heavy iteration costs more. — [NxCode comparison 2026 (secondary)](https://www.nxcode.io/resources/news/lovable-vs-bolt-new-2026-ai-app-builder-comparison)
- Lovable is the tool usually recommended to founders "who have never opened a terminal". Bolt suits people with some technical background. — [NxCode](https://www.nxcode.io/resources/news/lovable-vs-bolt-new-2026-ai-app-builder-comparison); [ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/vibe-coding-for-non-technical-founders-what-theyre-actually-building-in-2026)
- Non-technical founders commonly report getting about 80% of a working app almost instantly. The remainder (edge cases, security, scaling) is where they struggle. — [ValueAdd VC 2026](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/vibe-coding-for-non-technical-founders-what-theyre-actually-building-in-2026)
- Lovable's scale: about $400M ARR in Feb 2026 and about $500M by mid-2026. Bolt reached about $40M ARR within 5 months of launch. These are estimates, not audited. — [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/lovable.dev); [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/au/lovable-vs-bolt-new-vs-v0-2026/)
- One blog cites a non-developer who built a virtual try-on tool on Lovable that reached $800k ARR in 9 months. I could not verify this from a primary source. — [NxCode](https://www.nxcode.io/resources/news/lovable-vs-bolt-new-2026-ai-app-builder-comparison)
- **Security:** CVE-2025-48757 covers insufficient Supabase Row-Level Security in Lovable-generated apps through 15 Apr 2025, which let unauthenticated users read or write tables. A scan of 1,645 showcase apps found 170 vulnerable apps (≈10%) and 303 exposed endpoints, leaking PII, addresses and API keys. Lovable states that users are responsible for security. — [Superblocks](https://www.superblocks.com/blog/lovable-rls-breach); [CVE record](https://cvemon.intruder.io/cves/CVE-2025-48757)
- In Feb 2026, a Lovable-built app reportedly exposed 18,697 user records. I could not verify this from a primary source. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/serenitiesai/lovable-app-exposes-18000-users-the-vibe-coding-security-crisis-nobody-saw-coming-jo6)
- Stripe Atlas: 20% of new companies charged their first customer within 30 days (vs 8% in 2020). — [secondary summary of Stripe 2025 letter](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/stripe-2025-annual-letter-key-takeaways)
- French freelance developer day rates: Malt's average for freelance developers is cited by third parties at about 574 €/day (year unclear, possibly 2024). Secondary 2026 guides give about 485-560 €/day for full-stack and 350-450 € for juniors. — [Malt baromètre full-stack](https://www.malt.fr/t/barometre-tarifs/developpeurs/developpeur-backend/developpeur-fullstack); [La Fabrique du Net 2026](https://www.lafabriquedunet.fr/agences/tendances/tarifs-des-developpeurs-freelances-dans-les-grandes-villes-de-france)

### Inferences
- **Build budget with her 50 k€** (my estimate from the figures above):
  - Tooling (Lovable or Claude/Cursor-type tools, Supabase, hosting, domain): about 50-150 €/month.
  - Freelance help for security review, auth and multi-tenancy, and integrations: 10-25 days × ~500 €/day = 5-12.5 k€.
  - A credible B2B MVP therefore costs about 6-15 k€. That leaves 35-45 k€ for distribution, legal setup, insurance and runway.
- **Time:** 10 h/week is enough to prototype in 4-8 weeks. Selling to aerospace companies (security questionnaires, procurement) will consume most of those hours later. Her product, pricing and go-to-market skills are the scarcer asset.
- **Hosting constraints:** for export-control or ITAR-adjacent data, customers may require EU hosting, encryption and possibly ISO 27001 later. That raises the cost of serving customers well beyond the build cost.

### Gaps
- No independent data on what share of vibe-coded SaaS products reach paying customers or profitability.
- No sourced 2025-26 cost benchmark for a "Lovable MVP + freelancer hardening" package.
- Costs of ISO 27001 or SOC 2 for a micro-company were not researched.

## 4. "AI agents as a service" and vertical AI: what sells to SMBs and B2B in 2025-2026, pricing, and small teams with meaningful revenue

### Takeaway
Enterprise generative-AI application spend is large: $37B total in 2025, more than $19B on applications, and $4B on coding alone. Money concentrates in coding, support and role-specific copilots. Pricing is moving to hybrid models: a seat or platform fee plus a per-outcome charge (about $0.50-2 per resolved support ticket). Enterprise vertical agents sign six-figure contracts. Documented bootstrapped small-team successes are mostly horizontal tools (Base44, a vibe-coding builder), not niche B2B verticals.

### Cited Findings
- Menlo Ventures (Dec 2025, about 500 US enterprise decision-makers):
  - Enterprise generative AI spend was $37B in 2025, about 6% of the global SaaS market.
  - More than $19B went to applications.
  - Coding was the first "killer use case" at $4B, about 55% of departmental AI spend.
  - Copilots and assistants took about $7.2-8.4B.
  - Vertical AI covers healthcare, legal and others, but the excerpts give no dollar figures for verticals.
  - Sources: [Menlo Ventures 2025](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/); [No Jitter](https://www.nojitter.com/ai-automation/menlo-ventures-estimates-19-billion-in-gen-ai-spend-during-2025)
- Outcome-based pricing benchmarks: Intercom charges $0.99 per resolved conversation. HubSpot cut its Customer Agent price to $0.50 per resolution in April 2026. Zendesk is about $1.50-2.00 per resolution. Hybrid models (seat floor plus per-resolution charge) dominate in 2026. — [Pickaxe pricing guide 2026](https://pickaxe.co/post/ai-agent-pricing-models); [Korix 2026](https://korixinc.com/learning-center/ai-pricing-models-2026) (secondary guides)
- Enterprise vertical agents such as Sierra sign contracts of about $150-350K+/year. Leading vertical agents reportedly offer no self-serve free tier. Mid-market vertical agents are priced at about $50-500/month per agent. — [IdeaProof vertical AI agents guide](https://ideaproof.io/guides/vertical-ai-agents) (secondary)
- Outcome-based pricing is mainstream enough that Deloitte published accounting guidance for it on 4 June 2026. — [Deloitte DART](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/publications/deloitte/industry/technology/accounting-outcome-based-pricing-agentic-ai)
- An agency-side anecdote (n=34, low-authority blog): vertical AI agencies have a median deal of $14,200/month against $4,800 for horizontal ones. — [ForkOff](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/founder-growth/vertical-ai-agency-pricing-case-studies-2026)
- Typical vertical micro-SaaS profile: 1-3 founders, $50-500/month per customer, 70-85% gross margin. — [Growtoria (secondary)](https://growtoria.com/vertical-micro-saas-ai-business-guide/)
- SMB usage pricing ranges from $0.02 to $1.50 per conversation, and costs can spike under usage pricing. — [Reinventing.ai SMB 2026](https://insights.reinventing.ai/articles/ai-agents-cost-performance-smb-2026-04-08)
- Small-team success: Base44 was bootstrapped by one founder and 8 employees. It sold to Wix for $80M after 6 months (June 2025) and reportedly reached $100M ARR by Mar 2026. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/18/six-month-old-solo-owned-vibe-coder-base44-sells-to-wix-for-80m-cash); [Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/tags/Base44)
- AI-native companies are 3x more likely to reach $1M ARR in 6 months (ChartMogul 2025). The Stripe top-100 AI companies took a median 11.5 months to $1M (2024 data). — [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-growth-the-odds-of-making-it/); [Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/inside-the-growth-of-the-top-ai-companies-on-stripe)

### Inferences
- **What sells:** agents that replace a measurable, repetitive, document-heavy task: support triage, classification, document drafting and checking, compliance screening. Buyers pay per outcome or per seat plus usage.
- **Best fit for Johanna's 10 h/week:** a productised "agent + human-in-the-loop review" for a regulated niche. Examples: ECCN/dual-use pre-classification, technical-document compliance checks, training-record audits. Pricing would be platform fees of about 200-2,000 €/month per SMB customer plus a per-item charge.
- **Two models to avoid:** a bespoke "AI agency", which is high-touch and needs her hours, and generic horizontal agents, which compete with HubSpot or Intercom at $0.50-1/resolution.

### Gaps
- No primary-source example of a bootstrapped, sub-5-person vertical B2B AI agent company in aerospace or compliance with disclosed revenue.
- Roundup claims such as "support agent to $1M ARR in 117 days" (Chatbase/Yasser Elsaid) appear only in SEO blog roundups and are unverified.
- No France/EU-specific data on SMB willingness to pay for AI agents (no Bpifrance or France Num barometer retrieved).

## 5. Concrete niches linked to her domain (aerospace MRO, training, export control / dual-use, AI Act, supplier quality, Toulouse ecosystem, PM and change tooling): players, gaps, sizing

### Takeaway
The strongest fit is **export control and dual-use compliance for aerospace and defence SMEs**. Regulation is complex and changing (EU 2021/821 Annex I updated in Sept 2025 and Sept 2026). The flows are large: €77.6B authorised dual-use trade in the EU in 2024, and 3,558 French authorisations worth €21.6B in individual licences in 2025, up 37% mainly in nuclear and aerospace. Incumbents (SAP GTS, Thomson Reuters, Descartes, E2open) target large enterprises, and AI classification tools are only appearing now. MRO software is a $7-9B market dominated by heavy ERPs (AMOS, OASES), so a solo founder can only enter as a narrow add-on. ATO/pilot-training record software already has many small vendors. AI Act deadlines moved to Dec 2027 and Aug 2028, which pushes demand out.

### Cited Findings
**Export control / dual-use (EU Reg. 2021/821, ITAR/EAR)**
- EU 2024 (Commission annual report):
  - Authorised dual-use trade was €77.6B, about 3% of extra-EU goods exports.
  - EU general export authorisation transactions: 89,388.
  - National general authorisations: 23,406.
  - Individual licences: 18,549.
  - Source: [Council doc ST-12011-2026](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-12011-2026-INIT/en/pdf)
- Older data (2022): €57.3B authorised and €0.98B denied. — [European Commission, Jan 2025](https://policy.trade.ec.europa.eu/news/report-highlights-eus-approach-export-controls-dual-use-items-2025-01-31_en)
- France 2025 (DGE report published 21 Aug 2026): 3,558 dual-use export authorisations issued, cumulative value of individual licences €21.6B, total individual licences up 37% vs 2024 mainly in nuclear and **aerospace**. The competent service is the SBDU (Service des biens à double usage). — [DGE](https://www.entreprises.gouv.fr/la-dge/actualites/exportations-de-biens-double-usage-publication-du-rapport-2026)
- Annex I control list: updated by delegated regulation in Sept 2025, with a further update adopted on 14 Sept 2026. Every update forces re-classification work. — [EC Trade, exporting dual-use items](https://policy.trade.ec.europa.eu/help-exporters-and-importers/exporting-dual-use-items_en)
- Market size estimates are inconsistent and come from low-authority publishers:
  - Export compliance software ≈ $1.75B (2025), with Europe ≈ 29.1% of revenue.
  - Dual-use compliance checker ≈ $1.14B (2024), 11.2% CAGR.
  - Narrower "trade compliance software" ≈ $528M (2025).
  - Sources: [Dataintelo export compliance](https://dataintelo.com/report/export-compliance-software-market); [Dataintelo dual-use checker](https://dataintelo.com/report/dual-use-export-compliance-checker-market); [Market.us](https://market.us/report/trade-compliance-software-market/)
- Incumbent vendors: Amber Road (E2open), Descartes, SAP, Oracle, MIC Customs Solutions, Integration Point (Thomson Reuters), OCR Services. — [Dataintelo](https://dataintelo.com/report/dual-use-export-compliance-checker-market)

## 7. Key risks: AI commoditization, distribution, and employment constraints (exclusivity, loyalty, IP when a salaried consultant builds a product in her client domain)

### Takeaway
French law lets her create a company while employed:
- An exclusivity clause cannot be enforced against her for 1 year when she creates a business (C. trav. L1222-5).
- The duty of loyalty still applies with no time limit: she must not compete with Sopra Steria Next or use its resources.
- Software created "in the exercise of her duties or following employer instructions" automatically belongs to the employer (CPI L113-9), even if written at home.

An aerospace compliance or MRO product built while she consults on the same topics for Airbus via Sopra Steria is therefore the highest-risk configuration. The biggest business risks are distribution (B2B aerospace sales at 10 h/week) and commoditization of thin AI wrappers.

### Cited Findings
**Employment law (France)**
- An exclusivity clause cannot be enforced for one year against an employee who creates or takes over a company, whatever the contract says (C. trav. L1222-5). After that year, the clause applies again: the employee must stop the activity or get a written waiver. In case of creation leave or part-time for creation, the protection runs until the end of that period. — [Le Coin des Entrepreneurs](https://www.lecoindesentrepreneurs.fr/clause-d-exclusivite/); [L-Expert-Comptable](https://www.l-expert-comptable.com/a/6710-levee-des-clauses-d-exclusivite-pour-une-creation-d-entreprise.html)
- The duty of loyalty applies throughout the employment contract with no clause needed. It forbids any activity that directly competes with the employer, whatever its volume and even outside working hours. Using the employer's equipment or working on the project during paid hours are also breaches. — [Legalstart 2026](https://www.legalstart.fr/fiches-pratiques/autoentrepreneur/auto-entrepreneur-cumul-activites/); [Portail auto-entrepreneur](https://www.portail-autoentrepreneur.fr/academie/cumul/obligation-loyaute-salarie)
- Case law cited by a law firm: the Cour de cassation treats a directly competing activity as serious misconduct (faute grave) that justifies dismissal. In another case, a court of appeal rejected a dismissal for gross misconduct because the business creation fell within the protected one-year window. — [Kohen Avocats (loyauté)](https://kohenavocats.com/salarie-auto-entrepreneur-cumul-loyaute-exclusivite-durees-recours/); [Kohen Avocats (faute grave)](https://kohenavocats.com/obligation-loyaute-clause-exclusivite-cumul-activites-auto-entrepreneur-faute-grave/)
- Working-time limits on the combined activity: 10 h/day, 48 h/week, and 44 h/week averaged over 12 weeks. Service-public says combining a job with a micro-entreprise is possible if there is no exclusivity clause and no competition. — [Legalstart](https://www.legalstart.fr/fiches-pratiques/autoentrepreneur/auto-entrepreneur-cumul-activites/) (the service-public.gouv.fr page F11320 returned 404 when fetched)
- **IP (CPI L113-9):** economic rights to software created by employees in the exercise of their duties or following the employer's instructions vest automatically in the employer, with no express assignment needed. — [Le Monde du Droit](https://www.lemondedudroit.fr/pi-tic/278-propriete-intellectuelle-industrielle/28890-devolution-a-lemployeur-des-droits-sur-les-logiciels-developpes-par-ses-employes.html); [Baumann Avocats, art. L113-9](https://www.baumann-avocats.com/CPI/article-l113-9-du-code-de-la-propriete-intellectuelle.php)
- The CA Rennes (1 July 2014) held that working on the tool from home did not exclude L113-9. — [Doctrine, CA Rennes 2014](https://www.doctrine.fr/d/CA/Rennes/2014/R0B73F0CB038F147C35DA)
- L113-9 covers software written within the scope of the job. Software written by a founder on a personal basis still needs a proper assignment or licence to her own company. — [Seban Associés](https://www.seban-associes.avocat.fr/?p=109411)

**Commoditization and distribution**
- AI-native companies win early milestones faster, but fast acquisition can come with fast churn. Fewer than 1% of AI-native companies reach $10M ARR in 12 months, and vertical SaaS rarely produces outliers. — [ChartMogul 2025](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-growth-the-odds-of-making-it/)
- Platform players are pushing agent prices down: HubSpot's agent dropped to $0.50 per resolution in Apr 2026. — [Pickaxe 2026](https://pickaxe.co/post/ai-agent-pricing-models)
- Incumbents already bundle AI classification, e.g. Thomson Reuters ONESOURCE AI HS/ECCN. — [Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/en/products/onesource-global-classification-ai/features)
- EU founders are disadvantaged: US AI application firms were 2.2x as likely as EU firms to reach $1M within 12 months (Stripe, 2026 analysis). — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/us-ai-app-firms-2-2x-likelier-than-eu-peers-to-reach-1m-stripe-finds/)
- Liability and accuracy: LLMs missed a critical ECCN sub-entry in a practitioner test. — [Traliance](https://traliance.com/unleash-the-power-of-ai/)
- Security: about 10% of scanned Lovable showcase apps exposed data (CVE-2025-48757). — [Superblocks](https://www.superblocks.com/blog/lovable-rls-breach)

### Inferences
- **The Sopra Steria Next conflict is concrete.** As a consulting manager in product and innovation, often staffed on aerospace clients, any aerospace software product could be argued to:
  1. compete with Sopra Steria's aerospace consulting or software offers (loyalty);
  2. fall under L113-9 if it overlaps her assignments or uses client know-how;
  3. breach client confidentiality or trade secrets (secret des affaires, loi 2018-670, from general knowledge).
- **Mitigations** (general practice, to validate with a labour and IP lawyer):
  - Read her employment contract and the Syntec collective agreement for exclusivity, IP and non-compete clauses.
  - Ask for a **written authorisation** that describes the project's scope.
  - Build only outside working hours, on personal equipment and accounts, with no client data or documents.
  - Choose a niche her employer does not sell (e.g., an SME dual-use compliance tool rather than Airbus-scale MRO).
  - Document dated authorship (git history) and assign the IP to her own company (SAS/SASU).
  - Consider the legal creation leave or part-time for business creation (congé / temps partiel pour création d'entreprise) when moving to 4/5. This also extends the exclusivity protection.
- **Distribution risk dominates.** At 10 h/week, enterprise aerospace sales cycles are hard. Prefer self-serve or low-touch SMB pricing (≤ 500 €/month), content and SEO through a regulatory tracker or newsletter, and channel partners such as Aerospace Valley, CCI export advisers and customs brokers.
- **Commoditization defence.** Proprietary structured data (curated Annex I/CCL mappings, change history), workflow and audit trails, and regulatory trust are what an LLM call cannot copy. A pure "prompt over ChatGPT" product will be squeezed by incumbents and platforms within 12-24 months (my inference from pricing trends).
- **Liability.** Export classification errors can bring sanctions on the customer. The product needs "decision support, human validates" terms, professional liability insurance (RC Pro), and possibly a partnership with a customs or export-control consultancy.

- I could not check whether Sopra Steria's own contracts or internal policies include an exclusivity or IP-assignment clause beyond the law, or what the Syntec agreement says.
- Rules on creation leave and part-time for business creation (conditions, notice, employer's right to postpone) were not researched in detail.
- No quantitative data on churn or price erosion for AI-wrapper SaaS in 2026.
